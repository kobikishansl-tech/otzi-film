# DYATLOV PASS — script & facts

Film: red paper-cut documentary, 396 s (6:36), 1920×1080, 24 fps (figures 12 fps).

## Verified facts (sources)

- Nine ski hikers from the Ural Polytechnic Institute (Sverdlovsk), led by **Igor Dyatlov (23)**, died on the eastern slope of **Kholat Syakhl** ("Mountain of the Dead" in Mansi) on the night of **1–2 February 1959**; tent cut from inside, group fled under-dressed into ~−25 °C cold and wind. (Wikipedia: Dyatlov Pass incident)
- **Yuri Yudin**, the tenth member, turned back ill on 28 January at Vizhay — the last survivor's link; his farewell photo is famous. (Wikipedia; An Unknown Compelling Force, 2021)
- Soviet investigation (closed 1959): six died of hypothermia, three of fatal trauma (skull/chest, no external wounds); conclusion: a "compelling natural force". (Wikipedia)
- Tent found **26 February 1959**, cut open, empty. The last four bodies (in the ravine under the snow, by a stream) were found in **May**. (Wikipedia; Smithsonian, 2021)
- Theories: avalanche, katabatic wind, infrasound, ball lightning/UFO, military test, conflict with local Mansi, crime. (Wikipedia)
- **2020:** Russia reopened the case (prosecutor **Andrey Kuryakov**): avalanche most likely; quote: "It was a heroic struggle. There was no panic, but they had no chance." (Reuters/2020 coverage)
- **2021:** **Gaume & Puzrin, *Communications Earth & Environment* 2, 10 (2021)** — a **delayed slab avalanche**: the step cut into the slope for the tent + hours of wind-driven snow deposition above the shoulder → delayed slab release; impacts consistent with the compression injuries; small slab leaves little debris; nearby small slab avalanches later documented. Quotes: "We do not claim that now we have a final explanation… we added plausibility to the avalanche theory." (paper + ETH Zurich page)
- Cedar: two climbed it, branches broken up to ~5 m; two found by the fire; three (Dyatlov, Kolmogorova, Slobodin) died attempting to return to the tent; four in the ravine (Dubinina, Thibeaux-Brignolles, Zolotaryov, Kolevatov). (Wikipedia/autopsy summaries)

Tone: documentary, respectful — real people, no monsters, no gore; injuries described, never shown.
