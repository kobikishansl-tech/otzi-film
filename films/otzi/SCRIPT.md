# The Man the Glacier Kept — script and facts

## Facts (with sources)

- A man — now called Ötzi — died in the Ötztal Alps at about 3,210 m, around 3300 BC (~5,300 years ago); his body was preserved in glacier ice. Source: South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology (iceman.it).
- He was found on 19 September 1991 by two German hikers, face down in the melting snow of a gully. Source: South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology.
- He carried: a copper axe, a bearskin cap, a grass cloak, leather shoes with bearskin soles and grass netting. Source: South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology.
- His last meal, from stomach contents (46–50% animal fat): dried ibex meat and fat, red deer, einkorn wheat, traces of toxic bracken. Source: Maixner et al., *Current Biology* (12 July 2018), Eurac Research.
- He was murdered: an arrowhead lodged in his left shoulder, tore the subclavian artery (found on CT in 2001). Source: South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology.
- He was about 45 years old, ~1.60 m, with 61 tattoo groups. Source: South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology.

## Narration (English, ~105 words → ~28 s spoken; film ~55 s)

1. (0:00) Five thousand three hundred years ago, a man climbed an alpine pass and never came down.
2. He carried everything he owned: a copper axe, a bearskin cap, shoes stuffed with grass.
3. His last meal was ibex meat and fat, einkorn bread — eaten in a hurry, high on the mountain.
4. Then, in the snow of the pass, an arrow found his shoulder.
5. The snow of the same night buried him where he fell.
6. Luckily, it never let him go.
7. The ice kept his whole daily life — his axe, his hat, his last meal — for five thousand three hundred years.
8. In 1991, hikers found him lying face down in the melting snow, still wearing his cap.
9. He set out to cross the pass. He is still there — behind glass in Bolzano.
