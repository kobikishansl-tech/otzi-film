# TREATMENT — The Man the Glacier Kept (Ukiyo-e, ~55 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps)

## Logline and arc

A Copper Age man crosses an alpine pass with everything he owns; an arrow kills him, the snow buries him — and the snow that buried him keeps his daily life intact for 5,300 years until hikers find him face down in the melt. Setup: a man and his gear, alive. Turn: the arrow, then the snow that "luckily never let him go". Ending echoes the opening: the same pass, the same hat, now behind glass in Bolzano.

## Three candidate structures

1. **The handscroll journey** — one long scroll slides right→left through stations of his last day (village → gear → meal → climb → arrow → snow). Classic travel grammar; risks reading as a slideshow and the demo's own travel move.
2. **Alternate impressions of one view** (CHOSEN) — one constant subject, the pass with its gully, reprinted with different colour blocks: autumn day → dusk (the arrow) → snow (paper erases the print) → glacier blue → 1991 discovery. The story is told by *where the man sits in the frame* and which blocks land. Uses the medium's own grammar (a series of views + paper is the white + alternate impressions) and the snowfall is the native move at the emotional peak.
3. **A print being made, block by block** — key line = the man, blue block = the ice, red = the arrow; each block a chapter. Clever but abstract; buries the daily-life warmth the user asked for.

Why 2: the topic is preservation — the same place, unchanged, holding one life. Reprinting one view is exactly that, in the medium's own language.

## Benchmark

- **Learn**: Hiroshige's snow prints — snow as unprinted paper, distant layers pale, figures tiny; Hokusai's *Thirty-six Views* grammar — one subject, recomposed each time; *Mononoke* (2007) — print texture alive in motion.
- **Don't take**: any specific print (no Great Wave, no Sudden Shower over Ōhashi), characters, melodies, compositions.

## Subtitles and titles

- Narration lines as horizontal cartouches (cream slip, thick+thin border, small red seal left), Shippori Mincho 500 in sumi, centred bottom, ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
- View cartouche (vertical): series title "Views of the Pass" + per-view titles in a brush font; seal stamps on views 1 and 8.

## Native move

**Paper is the white** at the peak (33–41): the snowfall literally unprints the scene, leaving the seal — the image of being buried and preserved in one gesture.
